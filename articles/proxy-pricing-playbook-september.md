# Proxy Pricing Playbook - September 2024 

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-09-15 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/proxy-pricing-playbook-september?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Comparing proxy prices is harder than it looks. Every vendor bills in its own way: per GB, per IP, per request, or with credits and plans. Still, if you buy proxies for a living, you need to know whether the market is getting cheaper and where the offers are changing. This is the first Proxy Pricing Playbook, a series where every three months I check how proxy costs are moving.

We collected prices from the public pricing pages of the main providers and compared them with the July snapshot. The categories are datacenter, residential, ISP, mobile and unblocker proxies. To keep the comparison fair, I left out APIs that include parsing, such as Zyte API, and ignored coupons and offers not shown on the pricing page. Plans and pay-as-you-go prices are kept separate, and monthly prices are used when a plan can also be billed annually.

## What's inside

- How we handle vendors that sell the same product both as a plan and as pay-as-you-go, and why they get two rows in the spreadsheet.
- ISP proxies follow two pricing models, per GB and per IP, and the per-IP price depends on whether the IPs are shared or dedicated.
- Unblockers are billed per GB or per request, and Zenrows credit-based plans are hard to compare with vendors that bundle proxies.
- A vendor I did not know had an unblocker, which will go into the next Unblocker Benchmark.
