# Crawl of Fame - July 2026

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2026-07-27 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/crawl-of-fame-july-2026?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Residential proxy networks are having a hard time. After IPIDEA, the FBI went after NetNut, one of the biggest names in the industry, and seized its domain along with two related ones. Then LG banned residential proxy SDKs from its webOS apps. If you buy or sell proxies, this changes what supply, pricing and onboarding will look like in the coming months.

In this month's Crawl of Fame I go through both sides of the news. I share my view on NetNut, a company I've worked closely with, and I guess where the market is heading: less supply, higher prices, stricter KYC. On the brighter side, we look at Nimble's and Oxylabs' funding rounds and the court decision that dismissed Google's DMCA lawsuit against SerpApi. I also cover Cloudflare's new default settings for AI crawlers and why I think pay-per-crawl won't work as designed. I close with my "Napster era for content" comparison.

## What's inside

- What I think went wrong at NetNut, and why I doubt the company was directly involved.
- Two predictions for residential proxies: the end of the race to the bottom on price, and KYC becoming the default for access.
- Where the Cloudflare defaults for new sites stand on search, training and agents, and the date they apply.
- Why the fees in the pay-per-crawl model don't add up, according to our own calculation.

**In the full article:** Links to the sources on each story, the Bright Data KYC screenshot, the reference to Jason Grad's piece on Massive and my full reasoning on the Napster comparison.
