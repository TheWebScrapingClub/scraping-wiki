# Web scraping from 0 to hero: Introduction to web scraping

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-10-22 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-from-0-to-hero-introduction?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Questions on our Discord server and on the webscraping subreddit keep showing the same gap: people lack the basics. Courses on machine learning and AI are everywhere, while web scraping material is mostly marketing that ends with "buy a commercial solution", or sits in hackers' forums. Most people also focus on one tool or one issue and miss the big picture. Being a professional means more than solving a technical challenge. You also need to know whether what you are doing is legal and whether a wiser way to extract the data exists.

That is why I started a free course, tagged WSF0TH, with a bi-weekly schedule. This first chapter defines web scraping and separates it from screen scraping and web crawling. It then looks at why demand is growing, with e-commerce growth, market intelligence and AI training data as drivers, and at the freelance and data marketplace options for earning from it. The last part covers legality: which types of data we can collect and how accessible they are.

## What's inside

- How web scraping differs from screen scraping and web crawling, with a short definition of each.
- Why the Google search trend and the growth in e-commerce sites since COVID-19 point to rising demand.
- Where practitioners can earn from scraping today: Upwork, Fiverr, Freelancer.com and data marketplaces, including my disclaimer about Data Boutique.
- The legal split between personal, copyrighted and sui generis data versus factual data, and between non-public and public data, including the role of Terms of Service.
