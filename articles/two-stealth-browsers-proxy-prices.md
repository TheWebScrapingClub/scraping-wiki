# Two stealth browsers just dropped. Also, your proxy provider might be overcharging you.

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2026-03-25 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/two-stealth-browsers-proxy-prices?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Standard headless Chromium gets detected, and proxy pricing is still hard to pin down. If you scrape at scale, you need a reference for what you should pay for proxies, and you need browsers that go beyond patching evasion one leak at a time.

This bonus edition covers three things. I updated the Proxy Price Benchmark, which my fleet of agents now refreshes every week straight from the vendors' pricing. We added two providers, Dataimpulse and AnyIP, so it tracks 27 in total. I also flag two stealth browser projects that came up on the Scraping News feed. Owl Browser is a purpose-built engine on Chromium (CEF) for high-volume automation. Rayobrowse is Rayobyte's open-source stealth Chromium, released from their production infrastructure. I describe what each one is and where it fits. We have not tested them yet; that comes in later issues of The Web Scraping Club.

## What's inside

- Owl Browser is presented as a full engine, not a Playwright wrapper, self-hosted and Docker-ready, with Python and TypeScript SDKs.
- Rayobrowse randomizes fingerprints at the browser level and connects over CDP, so it works with Playwright, Puppeteer, Selenium or custom scripts.
- I am considering a paid API for the benchmark data and a waitlist is open to gauge demand and use cases.
- The shift described is from patch-level evasion to full browser-level stealth, with both browsers approaching it from different angles.
