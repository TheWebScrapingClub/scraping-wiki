# The January 2023 recap for the Web Scraping industry

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-01-29 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-2023-january-news?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

The web scraping industry keeps producing more content: podcasts, vendor newsletters, webinars and YouTube channels. Most of it is content marketing, but when it gives real value it is worth your time. January also brought the first lawsuit of the year. Meta was involved again, personal data was scraped again, and the Facebook Terms and Conditions were apparently broken again. That is the old problem of data being public and still not free to take.

In this monthly recap I go through what caught my attention. I list the podcast, newsletter and webinar material from SOAX, Oxylabs and Zyte, plus two videos I liked: a side-hustle success story and an end-to-end data project built on Bright Data. I cover Meta's accusations against Voyager Labs and give my view on scraping personal data. I also look at Antoine Vastel's DataDome research on PhantomJS traffic, Umasi's two-part study of the Kasada virtual machine on Nike's website, and the most read post of the month, THE LAB #9 on OpenSea.

## What's inside

- Which vendor podcasts, newsletters and webinars I found worth following, and why content marketing can still be useful
- Why I consider personal data a no-go for scraping projects, and why you should work with a legal team if you do it anyway
- What the DataDome research says about PhantomJS still showing up in traffic years after its last update, next to Playwright and Puppeteer
- A preview of the February interview I'm preparing with Veritas, a contributor on deobfuscation and scraping
