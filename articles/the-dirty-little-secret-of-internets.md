# The Dirty Little Secret of Internet's Data

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-01-07 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/the-dirty-little-secret-of-internets?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Web scraping sits under many of the tools we use every day, and almost nobody admits it. Startups put AI on every pitch deck, while the practice that feeds it stays in the back office. Even the r/webscraping about section says the first rule is not to talk about it. That silence is not harmless for practitioners, because it leaves people without mentors, examples or shared practice.

I look at the two reasons I see behind the secrecy: legal ambiguity and a weak competitive edge. Then I follow the side effects, where lack of shared knowledge turns into lower data quality and higher costs. I also list the industries that could not function without scraping, and ask whether the practice can be stopped at all. I close with my view of the data market sitting behind scrapers and proxies, and why websites should sell their data instead of building walls.

## What's inside

- Why "more proxy servers" is often a symptom of knowledge debt, and how that debt ends up in product cost and margin.
- A comparison of how openly OpenAI staff and Shell drilling specialists describe their core skill on LinkedIn.
- Which industries are functionally dependent on scraping: LLMs, e-commerce, travel, market research and lead generation.
- Why anti-bot measures and regulation have not stopped scraping, and what market size figures for scraping tools and proxies suggest.
