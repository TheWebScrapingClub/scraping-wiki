# The Unit Economics of Proxy Providers

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-05-27 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/the-unit-economics-of-proxy-providers?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Proxy resellers look like a money machine: bandwidth bought for cents and sold for dollars, a markup that has attracted entrepreneurs, investors and a few shady players. If you buy proxies for scraping, it helps to know where the IPs come from and what a per-GB price really pays for.

We look at how providers source their inventory, through SDKs embedded in third-party apps and through bandwidth sharing apps such as Honeygain and Pawns.app. Then we cover how that inventory is sold: per GB, per time, or per concurrent IP or port. The cost side goes well beyond bandwidth: engineering, customer acquisition, support and compliance. Proxidize, a US mobile proxy provider running its own SIMs and modems, serves as the worked example with its own per-GB numbers.

## What's inside

- Why SDK-based sourcing scales fast and gives IP diversity, especially on mobile, but is opaque to the end users whose devices become nodes.
- Bandwidth sharing apps are more transparent because the user chooses to install them, but they scale slower and limit IP diversity by device type and location.
- Customer acquisition is the least obvious cost, and it can take a large share of revenue even for providers at scale.
- Why owning the SIMs, modems and IPs, rather than reselling third-party bandwidth or using SDKs, changes the economics, and why volume decides whether it works.
