# Web scraping in market research and competitive analysis

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-03-12 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-in-market-research?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Market research used to mean surveys, third-party reports and manual data collection. That is slow and expensive, and the scope stays narrow. Scraping competitor sites, marketplaces, review pages and forums replaces most of that with automated collection, and it is now a standard input for pricing and product decisions.

We go through the five uses we see most: price monitoring, product analysis, customer reviews and sentiment, inventory and availability tracking, and spotting market trends. We then list the habits that keep a market research pipeline usable over time, from maintaining scripts when sites change to validating the data and staying inside legal and ethical limits. On tooling, we name Python libraries such as Playwright, Scrapy, Requests and Selenium, and no-code options such as Import.io, ParseHub and Apify. We close with an e-commerce retailer that tracks competitor prices across marketplaces and feeds the result into its pricing.

## What's inside

- How competitor stock levels and availability data can be used to anticipate shortages and react to competitor moves
- Why sentiment analysis on scraped reviews, forums and social media feeds customer experience and reputation work
- Four best practices: updating scripts, validating and cleaning data, respecting terms of service and privacy rules, and limiting request frequency
- A split of the tooling between Python libraries and no-code scrapers
