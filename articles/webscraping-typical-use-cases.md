# Web Scraping typical use cases

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-02-07 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/webscraping-typical-use-cases?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Web scraping gets called a versatile tool, which says little until you look at who runs it and for what. Pricing teams, SEO specialists, recruiters, investors and researchers all automate data collection from public websites, so their decisions rest on current data rather than guesswork.

We go through ten typical use cases and, for each one, say who uses it and what data they collect: market research and competitive analysis, price monitoring and dynamic pricing, SEO and content marketing, lead generation, financial data, job market analytics, real estate, social media monitoring, academic research, and travel and hospitality. It is an overview, not a technical guide, so there is no code and no tool comparison. For market research, price monitoring and SEO, I link to the dedicated articles we wrote on each.

## What's inside

- What competitor tracking covers in practice: pricing, promotions, product descriptions and customer reviews.
- Which sources each team targets, such as SERPs, LinkedIn and company directories, job portals, listing sites, and hotel and flight pages.
- How price monitoring feeds dynamic pricing, in e-commerce and in travel.
- Which less obvious users appear on the list, including economists, property managers and academic researchers.

**In the full article:** Descriptions of all ten use cases with the data each one relies on, plus the links to our dedicated pieces on market research, price monitoring and SEO.
