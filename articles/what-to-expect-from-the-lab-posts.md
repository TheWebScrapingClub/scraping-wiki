# What to expect from The Lab posts in 2024

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-01-04 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/what-to-expect-from-the-lab-posts?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

The Lab is the series of The Web Scraping Club where we work on real issues on real websites: how to get past anti-bot X, how to make a bot indistinguishable from a human browsing. I started it because the only write-ups I found came from commercial vendors and ended with a pitch for their services. Some say talking openly about solutions only helps anti-bot companies fix their products. I think sharing does more good than harm, even if a solution stops working later.

Here I look back at the 35 Lab articles, written mostly in 2023, and at the three most read: the hRequests benchmark, the Anti-Detect Anti-Bot matrix and the early 2023 Cloudflare update. Targets ranged from OpenSea and Etherscan to Cloudflare, PerimeterX, Kasada and Akamai. Then I lay out 2024: The Lab goes from bi-weekly to weekly, and the matrix returns as a recurring issue.

## What's inside

- Why Lab articles and their code sit behind the paywall and in a private GitHub repository: they are the most time consuming to write and test.
- The 2023 run started with OpenSea and Etherscan, both behind Cloudflare at the time, and shows why solutions on real websites go stale.
- Fabien Vauchelles wrote a guest Lab on reverse-engineering a mobile app API with Charles Proxy and Android Studio.
- The Anti-Detect Anti-Bot matrix compared 5 solutions against 5 anti-bots, and the Cloudflare solution will probably need an update in 2024.

**In the full article:** The list of past Lab posts with links, the reasoning on keeping the code in a private repository, and the poll on what to cover next.
