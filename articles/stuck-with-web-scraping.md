# Stuck? More of the Same Won’t Do

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-05-20 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/stuck-with-web-scraping?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Companies usually call us after being stuck on a website for months. Sometimes it has been over a year since they last extracted the data, or sold the service that depends on it. Internal teams want to solve it themselves, pride gets involved, and talking openly about scraping is uncomfortable. So they wait until the water is in the living room. Meanwhile a service sits unsellable and days turn into quarters.

I argue for a managerial approach. Before anything breaks, list every source you extract from and define two things for each: the cost of no service and the maximum time to escalation. Then I explain why technical challenges should never become personal, and what an independent opinion under NDA can change. I also cover what we see in our projects: advisors try different access paths, are not bound by your stack, and have time to test more tools.

## What's inside

- Where the cost of no service comes from: lost contracts, SLA breaches and service disruption, with limits defined in weeks and a stopwatch started when the issue arises.
- Why scraping's DIY culture turns blocks into personal contests with anti-bot systems, and why that is a problem for a business.
- Both outcomes of external advice are a win: it either restores extraction and trains your team, or it confirms a site is not feasible within your budget and throughput.
- How your own stack (language, proxy provider, orchestration, scheduler) can be the constraint, and why an outside voice can say what the team will not.

**In the full article:** Examples of the fixes we found from outside the client's stack, the full reasoning behind each of the three advantages of an external advisor, and the closing risk framework for managers.
