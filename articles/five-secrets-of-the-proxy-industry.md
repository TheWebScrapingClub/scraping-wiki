# Five Secrets of the Proxy Industry

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-03-18 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/five-secrets-of-the-proxy-industry?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Proxy vendors sell on big numbers: millions of IPs, exclusive pools, a price that depends on who is asking. When your success rate drops on a target like Shopee, those claims tell you little about what you can actually use. Picking a provider on them costs money and leaves you debugging blocks you can't explain.

I'm hosting Julia, who worked in sales at Bright Data and NetNut and now co-owns a proxy provider, for five secrets of the industry. She covers what an IP count really measures, how much providers' networks overlap, how to spot resellers and what to check before buying from one, why prices differ by customer region, and why exclusive IPs rarely hold up at scale. I also added a note pointing to the proxy price comparison tool I built for checking vendor prices.

## What's inside

- The metric to ask a provider for instead of the total IP count, and how rotation and cooling-off periods affect the IPs you can really use.
- Two ways different brands end up selling the same IPs: white-labeled networks and shared SDK publishers.
- What to check before buying from a reseller: uptime guarantees, community reviews, and what to ask when servers sit in between.
- Negotiation tactics for trials and pricing, and why long-term contracts are risky when proxy prices keep falling.
