# Introducing the Web Scraping 101 Wiki

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-02-19 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/introducing-the-web-scraping-101?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

The Web Scraping Club already has in-depth articles, interviews and a monthly news recap. Talking with the community, I felt one thing was missing: a common knowledge base about web scraping. Hundreds of tutorials answer "What is web scraping?", but a free and unbiased education project can't skip the basic questions people ask when they approach this industry. Think of Wikipedia: plenty of pages explain who Napoleon was, and it still has its own.

Here I introduce the Web Scraping 101 Wiki and how it will be run. One author can't cover all of web scraping, so domain experts and partners of the Club will pick a topic and write it down in a curated environment. I describe the Notion setup behind it: approved topics, a list of authors and a place for suggested topics. I also explain why wiki pages need a consistent format, what a page looks like, and how you can take part.

## What's inside

- Why a wiki fits a community that works through Discord and contributed articles, and not through a top-down knowledge base.
- The three public Notion databases: approved topics with their status, approved authors, and suggested topics.
- How an author picks an uncovered topic and asks to write it, and how to apply as an author.
- Why a common format is needed across authors, and that a template and writing guidelines are coming.

**In the full article:** The links to the Notion databases, the example wiki pages already written, and the contact details for applying as an author.
