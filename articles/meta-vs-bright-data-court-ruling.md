# Legal Zyte-geist #3: What the court’s ruling in the Meta v Bright Data case really means for web scrapers

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-02-13 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/meta-vs-bright-data-court-ruling?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Meta sued Bright Data for scraping Facebook and Instagram, and the court found that Meta's terms of service do not prohibit scraping public data. The headline travels fast and gets read as a green light for public data anywhere. That is the reading to be careful with, because the ruling rests on a specific set of facts and on the exact wording of two sets of terms.

In this edition of Legal Zyte-geist, Sanaea Daruwalla, Zyte's Chief Legal & People Officer, goes through what the court decided and what it left open. She covers the facts the court relied on, how it read the terms for logged-in versus logged-out use, and what it said about getting around anti-bot measures and CAPTCHAs compared with scraping behind a login. The point is how far the order reaches for those of us who scrape public data.

## What's inside

- How the court read "user" and "use" in the terms, and why Bright Data's corporate accounts did not bind its logged-out scraping.
- The court's view that defeating anti-bot tech and CAPTCHAs is not the same as piercing a login, and why that commentary is not a final judgment.
- Why a different court could reach a different result on other websites' terms for non-logged-in scraping.
- Which questions remain open: enforceability of terms against public data scraping and anti-bot circumvention under other laws.

**In the full article:** The complete list of facts the court relied on, the detail of its reading of Meta's terms, and links to Zyte's post on the ruling and its compliant web scraping checklist.
