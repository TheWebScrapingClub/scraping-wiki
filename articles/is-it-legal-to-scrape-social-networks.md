# Is it legal to scrape social networks like Facebook or Instagram?

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-02-16 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/is-it-legal-to-scrape-social-networks?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Social networks hold sensitive personal data, so scraping Facebook, Instagram, Twitter or LinkedIn calls for more caution than scraping a product catalog. Platforms tolerate scraping very little, especially after scandals like Cambridge Analytica. Legal trouble can come from two directions: the platform's Terms of Service and the privacy laws that cover the people whose data you collect.

I wrote a short introduction to both topics. On the ToS side, I explain why platforms are strict, how API access for selected partners fits in, and what changes when data is reachable without logging in. I use the HiQ vs LinkedIn case to show that even a legitimate scraper can pay a high price in time and money to prove it. On the privacy side, I cover why the laws of every country involved matter, not only your own, and what that means for the data you store and resell. It is a starting point, not legal advice.

## What's inside

- Why public data reachable without a login is treated differently from data behind an account, and the limits of that idea
- What the HiQ vs LinkedIn case says about the right to scrape public information and how long the legal fight took
- Why scraping from one country can still bring foreign privacy laws into play, with the Facebook example involving GDPR
- Why collected personal data should be anonymized and synthesized before it is sold, for example for sentiment analysis
