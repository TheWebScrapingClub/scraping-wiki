# Is web scraping legal?

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-02-07 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/is-web-scraping-legal?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Web scraping is not illegal in itself, but whether a given project is legal depends on how we collect the data, which data it is and what we do with it afterwards. The limits come from several directions at once: website terms of service, intellectual property rights, privacy regulations and, in the U.S., the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act. A wrong call can end in a lawsuit or a cease-and-desist order.

I go through these four factors one by one and read them against two U.S. court cases, HiQ Labs vs. LinkedIn (2019) and Craigslist vs. 3Taps (2013). Then I list practical guidelines for staying compliant, covering terms of service, personal data, robots.txt and asking permission, and a short set of ethical practices that go beyond what the law requires, such as how we pace requests and when to prefer an API.

## What's inside

- Why courts have not agreed on whether website terms of service can be enforced against scrapers, and when they treat them as too broad or ambiguous.
- How GDPR in Europe and CCPA in California make extracting personal data without consent a legal problem.
- What the CFAA means for publicly available data compared with data behind a password or an explicit restriction.
- What happens when scraping continues after a site has explicitly banned the scraper.
