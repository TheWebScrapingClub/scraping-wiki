# Four years of The Web Scraping Club

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2026-08-16 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/four-years-of-the-web-scraping-club?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

I started The Web Scraping Club in August 2022, in a hotel room in Tuscany, bored by the rain. It was meant as a way to write down my scraping notes in a more structured way, and I never imagined anyone would read it. Four years later more than 7,000 people receive it every week, with no marketing and no ads. Both the newsletter and the industry look very different from where they started.

I take stock of where TWSC stands today, starting with the Google SEO problem that made the whole domain vanish from search results, and what we released this year. Then I move to the industry. LLMs made the coding part faster, but the bigger change is how the world now sees web data and browser automation. I finish with what I want to build next and what stays the same in how I write.

## What's inside

- The fix I am trying for the SEO problem: a new domain address, based on my own experiments
- The tools released this year: a new version of the Proxy Price benchmark tool and the Scraping Wiki, a repo that organizes our findings as a second brain
- Ideas still in progress: a job board for open scraping positions, a revamped YouTube channel and a web scraping event in Italy in October
- Why recent funding rounds at Nimble and Oxylabs show the changed perception, and why a proxy company raising money before 2023 would have been unusual

**In the full article:** Details on the new domain, the links to the Proxy Price benchmark tool and the Scraping Wiki repo, my open question about the job board, the conferences I will attend and my closing promises on how TWSC will keep being written.
