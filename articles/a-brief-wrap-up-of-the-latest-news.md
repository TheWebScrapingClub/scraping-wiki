# A brief wrap up of the latest news on web scraping

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2022-08-12 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/a-brief-wrap-up-of-the-latest-news?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Legal and anti-bot news moves fast, and some of it changes how we work. This wrap-up covers three items. The first is a Korean Supreme Court ruling on whether scraping publicly available data from a competitor's website breaks the law. The second is the merger of two anti-bot vendors, PerimeterX and Human Security, which matters to anyone who runs into their protections. The third is a Kasada report on bots used against online pharma accounts.

We go through the Korean case, No. 2021Do1533, decided on May 12, 2022. It involved the travel agency Yanolja and GC Company, and the charges that followed the scraping of accommodation prices through an API. We follow the case from the first instance to the appeal and then to the Supreme Court. We then report the merger announced on July 27 and what the two CEOs said about it. Last, we cover Kasada's findings on brute force attacks with stolen credentials on pharma websites, presented at Black Hat in Las Vegas.

## What's inside

- Which charges the first-instance court upheld against GC Company, and how the appellate court and the Supreme Court dealt with them
- Why the court weighed browsewrap terms of use, the lack of technical blocks and the nature of the data on the API server
- How PerimeterX and Human Security came together and the reasons the CEOs gave for the merger
- What Kasada says attackers want from pharma accounts, and which substances they target
