# The X vs Bright Data case

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-07-09 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/x-vs-bright-data-case-scraping?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Bright Data keeps getting lawsuits from social media giants dismissed, and it is tempting to read each dismissal as a green light for scraping public data. It is not that simple. X sued Bright Data over the scraping of its public content and over the tools Bright Data sells to do it, claiming breach of its terms of service, trespass and fraudulent business activity. For anyone running scrapers or proxy infrastructure, the details of the ruling decide what carries over to your own work.

We go through the court's most recent ruling in X v. Bright Data claim by claim: trespass, unlawful and fraudulent business activity, and breach of contract, split into unauthorized access and scraped data. For each one we look at what the court said, how narrow the reasoning is, and why the leave to amend means the case is not over. This is a legal column, not legal advice.

## What's inside

- How the court treated X's argument about diminished server capacity, both as injury for trespass and as damages for contract.
- What the Business and Professions Code claim means for proxy providers and IP rotation services.
- Why not being logged in did not settle whether a contract with X existed, and the role of actual knowledge of the terms.
- How copyright ownership of user-generated content and X's change to its terms during the lawsuit shaped the contract claim.

**In the full article:** Case citations and quoted passages from the ruling, the court's reasoning on each of X's claims, and the specific limits of the decision for scrapers.
