# Web scraping and journalism: the Chiara Ferragni case

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-07-07 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/chiara-ferragni-pandoro-dataset?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Pandoro gate, the scandal around Chiara Ferragni's charity campaign tied to a Christmas cake, has been one of the most followed news streams in Italy. Sponsors dropped her, and her clothing brand, which made 14 million EUR in 2022 mostly through wholesalers, became a story in itself. Press reports said shops could not sell her products and had to discount them heavily, but nobody showed a number. That is where history on web-scraped data becomes useful.

We took that claim and tried to measure it with historical datasets from Databoutique. At Farfetch, where individual boutiques set the prices, we compared the share of discounted items against other brands and tracked stock and item counts. Then we checked Yoox, an off-season site, to test whether boutiques were passing their inventory there, looking at item counts and discount levels. We also explain why we picked these channels and left out high-end luxury sites.

## What's inside

- The difference between in-season and off-season fashion websites, and why it matters when you pick sources for a discount analysis.
- Why Net-a-porter and Mytheresa are the wrong benchmark for an affordable brand, and why Farfetch works as a magnifying glass for the market.
- Farfetch exposes the exact number of available items in its code, while Yoox gives no inventory levels, so the two analyses have different depth.
- The brand came back to Farfetch only in August 2023, which limits how far back the comparison can go.
