#  A preview of the Zyte 2026 Web Scraping Industry report

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2026-01-25 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/a-preview-of-the-zyte-2026-web-scraping?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Every January brings industry reports, and Zyte gave us an early look at theirs, "The age of fast-forward web data". It opens with a market that passed $1 billion in 2025 and keeps growing. Web scraping is no longer a fringe practice. It has become economic infrastructure, and the six trends in the report matter to anyone who extracts data for a living.

We went through the report and pulled out what matters. The trends run from the end of standalone proxies to the arrival of AI in the toolchain, from agent-based pipelines to an accelerating arms race with anti-bot vendors. The last two cover a web split by access rules and new regulation. Where the report is convincing we say so. Where it needs context, such as buy versus make or how far agents are ready for production, we add our own view. We also connect the cost pressure on scraping to what we are building at Databoutique.com.

## What's inside

- Zyte's rule of thumb for choosing between LLM extraction and code generation, depending on volume and how often a site changes.
- How far agentic pipelines are from production, and why selective use on stable sources still makes sense.
- The three access lanes of the web (hostile, negotiated, invited) and why identity becomes central for bots.
- The two regulatory dates in 2026, California AB 2013 and the EU AI Act, and the litigation cited around AI training data.

**In the full article:** The market figures and vendor statistics cited per trend, the list of AI applications in the scraping toolchain, the multi-agent architecture, the protocols behind each access lane, and our comments and conclusions.
