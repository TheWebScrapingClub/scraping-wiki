# Web Scraping for SEO and content marketing

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2022-03-12 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-for-seo?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

SEO runs on accurate, timely data, and most of it sits on pages someone else controls. Competitor keywords, meta descriptions, headings, backlink profiles and SERP positions can all be checked by hand, but not at any useful scale or frequency. That is where scraping earns its place in a marketing team, and it is often overlooked.

I go through the main SEO use cases for scraped data: competitor keyword analysis, backlink analysis, rank tracking and SERP monitoring, content gap identification, local SEO citation tracking, and trend and sentiment analysis on social media, blogs, forums and news sites. Then I list the tool options, from Python libraries like BeautifulSoup, Scrapy, Requests and Selenium, to browser extensions and no-code platforms such as ParseHub, Import.io and Apify. I also cover ethical practices, and close with an example of an organic skincare business using scraped competitor data to find keyword gaps.

## What's inside

- How to choose between code, browser-based and no-code tools depending on technical skills, data needs and scale.
- What scraping SERPs lets you watch beyond rankings, such as featured snippets and other SERP features.
- Why local SEO relies on scraping directories and business listings to keep citations consistent.
- Three practices for scraping responsibly: respecting robots.txt, limiting request frequency, and updating scripts when sites change.
