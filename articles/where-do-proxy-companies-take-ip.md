# Where do proxy companies take residential IPs from?

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-02-02 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/where-do-proxy-companies-take-ip?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Every proxy company sells residential IPs, and new ones keep appearing, so the obvious question is where those IPs come from and how hard it is to enter the market. Julia Levi worked in sales at BrightData and NetNut and now co-founded ByteConnect, an SDK provider for proxy companies. In this guest post she answers from inside the industry, focusing on peer-to-peer residential rotating IPs, the type behind most successful scrapers and a good share of the lawsuits.

She explains how real user devices become exit nodes through SDKs embedded in apps, how app developers get paid, and what the privacy impact is. She separates transparent deployments from the murkier ones, and questions the claim that a whole pool comes from one app. She also covers how to spot a reseller and what all this means for the number of truly distinct IP pools.

## What's inside

- Why building a stable SDK is a real barrier to entry, and why getting apps to partner with you is a full-time job
- How app monetization through IPs compares with ads, and which metrics apps are paid on
- The practices that are not above-board: sticky SDKs, hidden SDKs, router or SIM card farming and botnets
- Why a proxy company that shows no SDK of its own is probably a reseller, and why that is not necessarily bad for your price

**In the full article:** Julia's full breakdown of the three proxy IP types, the SDK data flow from device to proxy server, the retention figure behind the one-app argument and her closing view on transparency.
