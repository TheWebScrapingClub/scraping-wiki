# The Browser Automation Landscape in 2025

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-02-09 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/browser-automation-landscape-2025?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Browserless tools like Scrapy are fast and handle concurrency well, but anti-bot systems spot them easily unless an Unblocker API sits in front. Browser automation tools like Selenium, Playwright and Puppeteer can imitate a human, but they are slow and expensive. In my projects I avoid them whenever I can, especially on medium or large websites. Anti-detect browsers improved the disguise, not the infrastructure, so the scalability limits stayed.

Now more companies sell managed browser automation built for scraping, and the AI demand for web data is pushing them. We map the three families: open-source frameworks and their stealth add-ons, anti-detect browsers, and managed browsers. For each we look at how it gets detected, who it fits, and where it stops scaling. We list the main anti-detect and managed browser vendors with their pricing models, and say when an Unblocker API is the better choice.

## What's inside

- Why Playwright on a server is flagged: no monitor, no GPU, no audio or video devices.
- Stealth patches for open-source tools, including undetected-chromedriver and Botasaurus, and why they are only a partial fix.
- Anti-detect pricing is driven by profile count, and the cost you carry is the machines that run the browser instances.
- Managed browsers are priced per GB or per concurrent session, and not all of them offer stealth: some trade it for speed or for AI agent use.

**In the full article:** The vendor lists with prices and plan limits for nine anti-detect browsers and eight managed browsers, plus the links to our past anti-detect and Unblocker benchmarks.
