# Monetize your web scraping skills: a brief guide

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-01-14 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/monetize-your-web-scraping-skills?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

I read the Almanack of Naval Ravikant over the holidays. One of its ideas is that the future of work is freelancing, because the internet lowers the friction between a company and the contractor it needs. Applied to web scraping, a professional today can pick a corporate career or an independent path, and the second one has more options than it used to.

I go through three of them. The first is the traditional freelance marketplaces such as Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer.com, with their pros and cons. The second is Apify, where you build actors and list them in the Apify Store under different pricing models. The third is selling the extracted data on a marketplace like Data Boutique, where I'm one of the co-founders, and I say so in the text. For each route I describe how the money works and what you have to do to start. I close with my view on why demand for web data keeps growing.

## What's inside

- How much demand there is on Upwork for web scraping jobs, and where else to find freelance offers.
- The practical downsides of freelancing: finding gigs, competing on price, understanding requirements and estimating costs.
- The three Apify pricing models for actors (free, rental, paid per result) and how the developer's share is calculated.
- What a seller has to prepare on Data Boutique, how bids and the catalog work, and the pricing trade-off against buyers scraping the data themselves.
