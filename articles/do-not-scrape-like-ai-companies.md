# Scrape like a pro... but not like an AI company

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2024-07-29 (for paid subscribers). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/do-not-scrape-like-ai-companies?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Web scraping has always sat in a gray area. Look at LinkedIn and try to find a "Head of web data acquisition" at OpenAI, Perplexity or Mistral. They all have scraping teams, but few say so in public. I expected the AI boom to legitimize our industry. Instead, I hear more and more people talking about scraping for the wrong reasons.

I start from the basics: scraping is just a tool, like a hammer, and behavior makes it legal or not. I recap the legal points from Sanaea Daruwalla of Zyte and the ethical rules we follow, including server load, robots.txt and not interfering with the target's business. Then I look at how AI companies behave against those rules. I cover the lawsuits over copyrighted material, user agents that were declared and then bypassed, and the complaints from site owners about bots pulling terabytes. I also look at what this means for sites that must buy bot mitigation, and for scrapers who stayed polite.

## What's inside

- Which legal points we keep from Zyte's guide: personal data, copyrighted material, and data behind logins and paywalls.
- Why a declared user agent and robots.txt instructions mean little when bots ignore them, and why robots.txt is still a matter of netiquette.
- The iFixit case: one million hits in 24 hours from a bot that was blocked in robots.txt, plus the bad engineering patterns site owners report.
- How data deals, such as Reddit with OpenAI and Databoutique, try to give site owners revenue, and why no such solution exists for news and social sites.

**In the full article:** Our full list of ethical scraping rules, the links to the lawsuits, the Reuters report and the iFixit complaint, and the details of the monetization options for site owners.
