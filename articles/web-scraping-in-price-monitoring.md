# Web Scraping in Price Monitoring and Dynamic Pricing

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2022-03-12 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-in-price-monitoring?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Online retail prices move fast, and buyers compare them in seconds. A company that can't see what competitors charge is pricing blind. That is the case for price monitoring, which tracks competitor prices and market trends over time, and for dynamic pricing, which adjusts your own prices based on demand, competitors, supply and consumer behavior.

I cover how scraping supports both: real-time competitive intelligence, finding optimal price points from historical data, protecting margins, and reading how consumers react to price changes when you combine price data with sales and reviews. Then I look at three sectors where this is common: e-commerce retail, travel and hospitality, and consumer electronics. I also group the tools you can pick from into Python libraries, commercial solutions and cloud platforms, and close with a short list of practices for scraping prices responsibly.

## What's inside

- Where scraped price data ends up in e-commerce, travel and hospitality, and consumer electronics, and what each sector collects
- How the choice of tool depends on scale, frequency and complexity of the scraping operation
- Three groups of tools: Python libraries, commercial scraping solutions and cloud-based platforms
- Four practices for ethical price scraping: robots.txt, scraping frequency, proxy rotation and data compliance
