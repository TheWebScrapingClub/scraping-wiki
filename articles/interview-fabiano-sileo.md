# Interview #8 - Fabiano Sileo

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-05-07 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/interview-fabiano-sileo?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Most of what we cover here is scraping itself. This month I wanted the view from the other end of the pipeline, from someone who turns data into decisions. Fabiano Sileo works in business intelligence and data at Verisure, runs a podcast and a newsletter in Italian, and started as a SAP BW consultant. His take on what BI is, and on how little web data small and medium-sized Italian companies use, matters if you sell or build scraping projects.

We talked about his career path from technical consulting to financial planning and reporting. Then we moved to the questions that decide whether a data project gets funded or ignored. I asked how he defines business intelligence, whether data projects are still hard to sell when ROI is hard to measure, and how he builds a dashboard that decision makers actually read. We also covered tools for cleaning, enriching and monitoring data on its way to the end user. As usual, I closed with a fun fact from his early career.

## What's inside

- His definition of BI: turning raw data into information, then removing the noise so the decision maker sees only what matters.
- A real estate agency example showing why internal KPIs alone cannot separate business performance from market context, and what web data adds.
- Why ROI alone is a weak argument for a data project, and which other arguments he uses instead.
- His dashboard process in Power BI, from listing metrics to sketching a mockup before building anything, and his view of the data warehouse as the core of storage and enrichment.

**In the full article:** Fabiano's complete answers, his full list of steps for designing a dashboard, the arguments he uses to justify data projects and the story of his first day as a BI consultant.
