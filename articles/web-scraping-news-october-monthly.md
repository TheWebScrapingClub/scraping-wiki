# Web Scraping News: October Monthly Recap

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2022-10-30 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/web-scraping-news-october-monthly?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Every month some scraping news ends up in court or in front of a regulator, and it changes what we can safely build. In October the cases were about login walls, consent and biometric data. Meta settled its lawsuit against BrandTotal and Unimania, two companies that collected Facebook and Instagram data through browser extensions. In France, Clearview AI got another GDPR fine, after Italy and the UK.

I recap three stories from the month. The first is the Meta settlement: who the two companies were, how the extensions worked, and what they agreed to in the filing. The second is the Clearview fine and the CEO's reply, which I comment on from a legal angle, mainly around consent under the GDPR versus US law. The third is an Oxylabs white paper on web data adoption in e-commerce in the UK and US, with a couple of headline figures on how widespread scraping is in the sector.

## What's inside

- Which data the UpVoice and Ads Feed extensions collected from users who installed them, and why that mattered for the case.
- What the settlement obliges BrandTotal and Unimania to do besides paying confidential damages.
- My reading of the Clearview CEO's statement and what it implies for compliance in Europe.
- The difference between scraping the open web in the US and collecting data without consent under the GDPR.

**In the full article:** Details of the Meta settlement terms, the Clearview fine amounts by country with the CEO's full statement, the white paper's adoption and revenue figures, and the link to download it.
