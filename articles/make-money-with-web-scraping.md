# Three ways to make money with web scraping as a freelancer

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2025-04-21 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/make-money-with-web-scraping?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Freelance platforms used to be the only way for a scraper developer to earn. They still are the first one, and the competition is global and fierce. You are mostly trading time for tasks. The question is how to get more out of the same work once the gig is delivered.

I walk through three streams built on a single scraper. The first is custom projects on Upwork, Fiverr and Freelancer.com, including how to stand out in a crowded market. The second is selling the resulting datasets on Datarade, AWS Data Exchange and Data Boutique, and I compare what each does for discovery, delivery, quality control and payment. The third is selling the scraper itself as an Actor on the Apify Store or as an API on RapidAPI. As founder of Data Boutique, I say so openly and use it for a practical example, listing the Zillow dataset we built the week before.

## What's inside

- How a new freelancer without reviews can build credibility, and why niching down helps more than competing on everything.
- What each data marketplace does and does not handle: discovery, delivery, quality checks, payment. Datarade carries listing costs, AWS Data Exchange is free to join.
- Why a RapidAPI listing suits only scrapers that query a specific URL and do not run long, and why Apify requires an Actor version of your code.
- Two choices when listing on Data Boutique: how to price against a buyer who could do it themselves, and which refresh policy fits, from on demand to static to scheduled.

**In the full article:** The screenshots of the Data Boutique listing flow, the platform's fee and price range, the refresh policy options, and the account of how the Zillow dataset was matched to its schema and delivered through S3.
