#  Legal Zyte-geist #1: Step-by-Step Guide to Compliant Web Scraping

> Summary of an article published on [The Web Scraping Club](https://www.scraping.club/?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary) on 2023-12-05 (free to read). **[Read the full article](https://www.scraping.club/p/assessing-legal-compliance-of-web-scraping?utm_source=wiki&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=article_summary)**

Before starting a scraping project, you need to know whether it calls for a legal review. This first Legal Zyte-geist column is by Sanaea Daruwalla, Chief Legal & People Officer at Zyte. It turns that question into a checklist. You define what you scrape, from where and for what use, then go through the list. Any "yes" means a deeper legal look.

We apply the checklist to three real use cases: product data for competitive pricing intelligence, business executive data for a database, and large-scale extraction to train an LLM for a GenAI app. Along the way we cover personal data under GDPR and US state laws such as the CCPA, terms of service and logins, copyright, and AI regulation in the EU and US. It is not legal advice.

## What's inside

- Why a field like reviewer name changes the analysis for a pricing project, and how the US state treatment of public personal data differs from GDPR.
- Why US state thresholds matter when you build an executive database, and which CCPA wording on publicly available information applies.
- Where LLM training sits in the EU draft AI Act's risk-based approach, next to the US AI Executive Order and upcoming NIST guidelines.
- The two copyright questions on training and output, the state of the litigation against the big GenAI makers, and why a Legitimate Interest Analysis and a DPIA come up at this scale.

**In the full article:** The compliance checklist graphic, the CCPA passage, the checklist answers for each of the three use cases, and links to Zyte's resources on scraping legality and its detailed compliant scraping checklist.
